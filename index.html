Executive Summary

The National Grand Lodge of Greece (NGLG) fully respects the sovereign prerogative of every Grand Lodge to determine its own external relations. The issue addressed here is narrower and factual: a decision not to establish amity must not be presented as proof that another Grand Lodge is irregular.

The central defect in the text under review is the conflation of three distinct questions: regularity, bilateral recognition, and territorial recognition policy. They are related concepts, but they are not interchangeable.
1986Constituted by six Lodges previously regularly constituted under the Grand Lodge of Greece.
1993The United Grand Lodge of England extended recognition to the NGLG.
1994The Grand Lodge of Ireland recognised the NGLG.
1995The Grand Lodge of Scotland recognised the NGLG.
1999UGLE withdrew recognition; NGLG historical material records the critical qualification of its “continuing Regularity”.
TodayIreland and Scotland remain listed among the NGLG's mutual recognitions, alongside a substantial international network.

1. Regularity Is Not Recognition

A Grand Lodge may satisfy the recognised principles of regular Masonic origin and practice without being in amity with every other regular Grand Lodge. The absence, termination or withholding of a bilateral relationship therefore does not, by itself, determine regularity.

It is legitimate for a sovereign Grand Lodge to state: “We do not recognise the National Grand Lodge of Greece.”

It is a materially different proposition to state or imply: “The National Grand Lodge of Greece is therefore irregular.”

This distinction is indispensable to an accurate assessment of the Greek case.

2. The Historical Record

7 June 1986 — Foundation. The NGLG was constituted by six Lodges that had previously been regularly constituted under the Grand Lodge of Greece. Describing this history solely by the word “schism” does not determine the separate Masonic question of regularity of origin.

1993 — UGLE recognition. The fact of recognition is itself historically significant: NGLG was not treated as possessing an incurable defect of origin.

3 October 1994 — Ireland. The Grand Lodge of Ireland recognised NGLG.

4 May 1995 — Scotland. The Grand Lodge of Scotland recognised NGLG.

1999 — Change in UGLE recognition. UGLE ceased bilateral recognition. The NGLG's historical record emphasises the distinction between that policy decision and its continuing regularity.

3. Review of the Published Assertions

4. The Two Historic Home Grand Lodges

A complete account must acknowledge that the Grand Lodge of Ireland and the Grand Lodge of Antient Free and Accepted Masons of Scotland are listed by the NGLG among its mutual recognitions. Their relationship with NGLG is therefore not incidental attendance at an international event, but a matter of formal inter-jurisdictional recognition.

The omission is material because England, Ireland and Scotland occupy a distinctive historical place in organised regular Freemasonry. The present recognition position is therefore more complex than a binary description of “mainstream regularity” versus NGLG.

5. Exclusive Territorial Jurisdiction

The NGLG does not dispute the sovereign right of an American Grand Lodge to use territorial considerations when determining its own relations of amity. The objection is to converting such a policy into a universal test of regularity.

The proper conclusion is narrower: a jurisdiction may consider an existing recognition incompatible with extending a second recognition in the same territory under its own policy. That explains its decision concerning amity; it does not, without more, adjudicate the intrinsic regularity of the other Grand Lodge.

6. International Standing

NGLG documentation records an extensive network of international recognitions, continued relations with Scotland and Ireland, and sustained participation in international Masonic fora. These facts do not compel any sovereign jurisdiction to grant recognition. They do, however, make an unqualified portrayal of NGLG as lying outside regular international Freemasonry historically and institutionally incomplete.

The strongest and most accurate position is therefore not that UGLE presently recognises NGLG — it does not — but that current non-recognition by UGLE must not be misdescribed as a finding of irregularity.

7. Formal Conclusion

Requested correction of the record

The National Grand Lodge of Greece respectfully requests reconsideration of any published text that conflates recognition with regularity, omits the formal recognition maintained by Ireland and Scotland, or presents territorial exclusivity as a universal determination of Masonic regularity.

Any Grand Lodge remains entirely free to decline recognition. The historical record should nevertheless be stated completely and accurately so that the exercise of that sovereign discretion rests upon facts rather than an incomplete characterisation of the NGLG's Masonic standing.

The documentary record supports a clear distinction: non-recognition by a particular jurisdiction is a fact about the relationship between two sovereign Masonic bodies; it is not, without further evidence, a universal adjudication of regularity.
